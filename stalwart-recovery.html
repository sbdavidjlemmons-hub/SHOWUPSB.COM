Stalwart Recovery: Funding, Services, Outcomes, and Oversight

CONTRACT CEILINGS ≠ CASH PAID · REPORTED OUTCOMES ≠ INDEPENDENT EVALUATION · STRUCTURAL RISK ≠ PROVEN WRONGDOING

Prepared for: Show Up SB
Research cutoff: September 10, 2026
Entity reviewed: Stalwart Clean & Sober, Inc. (public-facing name: Stalwart Recovery)
EIN: 20-8188849
Evidence standard: official contract or filing; government-reported outcome; provider self-report; unknown

Executive finding

Stalwart Clean & Sober, Inc. is a Santa Barbara 501(c)(3) that provides recovery-residence and sober-living housing. Public records establish substantial Santa Barbara County contract authority, including an amended Behavioral Wellness contract ceiling of $1,089,850 for June 2024 through June 2027 and a separate County Probation ceiling of $84,500 for FY2026–27. Those figures are maximums, not verified cash paid.

Current Behavioral Wellness schedules label Stalwart’s recovery-residence services non-Medi-Cal billable. No direct CenCal Health or CalAIM contract or payment to Stalwart was located. Residents may receive off-site Drug Medi-Cal treatment from other providers while County, block-grant, opioid-settlement, BHBH, diversion, or AB109 funds pay for housing. Those roles must not be conflated.

County-reported performance is mixed. In FY2022–23, reported successful-discharge percentages exceeded a 50% goal, while retention and participation in three or more case-management services missed their goals in all three published quarters. For FY2024–25, the County reported 93 clients served, 84 discharged, and 64% successfully discharged, defining successful discharge as maintaining sobriety at discharge. No Stalwart-specific long-term sobriety, permanent-housing, employment, occupancy, waitlist, or recidivism result was found.

The strongest oversight concerns are documented but do not establish misconduct:

  • A 2023 County Board letter said Stalwart fell into the County’s “high risk” contractor category, but did not publish the assessment, score, factors, or deficiencies.
  • The FY2025 IRS filing reported $591,769 revenue, $582,165 expenses, $16,472 assets, $22,459 liabilities, and negative $5,987 net assets.
  • The same filing reported a family relationship between two officers, no written conflict-of-interest, whistleblower, or document-retention policy, and an $8,565 year-end related-party loan-table balance involving the executive director without reported board approval or a written agreement.
  • No public documentary finding of fraud, duplicate billing, licensing violation, sanction, repayment, lawsuit, or completed corrective-action plan was located.

The appropriate Show Up SB position is: recognize the housing service, verify the funding and outcomes, request the missing records, invite documented corrections, and never convert a structural risk into an accusation.

What can and cannot be added

  • The 2020–23 purchase-order and contract figures overlap and were superseded. Adding them would double count.
  • The $1,089,850 and $84,500 agreements cover different County programs but overlap in time and use overlapping Stalwart properties. They can be reported separately as contract ceilings; they should not be presented as a single cash-received total without payment records.
  • BWell’s 22-bed availability and Probation’s four guaranteed beds cannot be summed as 26 separate physical beds.
  • The $8 million Prop 47 award belongs to Santa Barbara County. No Stalwart subaward amount was identified.

3. CalAIM and CenCal finding

No direct CenCal Health or CalAIM contract, claim stream, or payment to Stalwart was located in the reviewed public sources. The current Behavioral Wellness schedule explicitly places Stalwart’s recovery-residence services under non-Medi-Cal billable services.

The contract requires residents to be engaged in off-site Drug Medi-Cal Organized Delivery System outpatient, intensive-outpatient, or recovery services delivered by eligible treatment providers. That creates a legitimate possibility that two organizations support the same person during the same month for different services: one for housing/room and board and another for covered treatment. It does not prove duplicate billing.

The correct audit unit is person + provider + service code or contract deliverable + bed/site + date + payer + invoice/claim status. Only transaction-level reconciliation can distinguish complementary services from duplicate payment.

5. Published utilization and short-term outcomes

The strongest Stalwart-specific actuals appear in County Board letters. They are government-reported contract data, not an independent evaluation.

FY2022–23 quarter Unique clients within quarter Retained in treatment Engaged in treatment Participated in 3+ case-management services Successfully discharged
Q1 33 30% 94% 21% 62%
Q2 43 29% 90% 22% 66%
Q3 26 0% 96% 8% 67%
Contract goal 34–69 clients 50% 100% 100% 50%

Interpretation:

  • The reported successful-discharge measure exceeded its 50% goal in all three quarters.
  • Clients served fell below the low end of the goal in Q1 and Q3.
  • Retention, treatment engagement, and case-management participation missed their goals in all three published quarters.
  • Do not add 33 + 43 + 26 and call it 102 annual unique people; “unique” applies only within each quarter and residents can span quarters.
  • The Board letter does not provide denominators or detailed operational definitions for the percentages.
  • “Successful discharge” is not evidence of sustained sobriety, permanent housing, employment, or reduced recidivism after exit.
  • A narrative sentence says 100% treatment engagement in Q3 while the detailed measure says 96%; the detailed table is the more specific source.

For FY2024–25, the County reported:

  • 93 clients served;
  • 84 clients discharged;
  • 64% of discharged clients successfully discharged; and
  • successful discharge defined as maintaining sobriety at discharge.

The Board letter says other care-coordination outcomes were unavailable because of the SmartCare EHR transition and describes subsequent staff training and feedback. This is a documented data gap, not proof of wrongdoing.

6. Staffing, standards, and licensing

  • The FY2025 Form 990 reports two employees. It lists Steve Goralski at 20 hours per week and $72,670 compensation, Kelly Berger at 20 hours and $6,582, and Marquel Goralski at one hour and $0.
  • The provider website says each property has a live-in house manager and assistants but provides no total headcount, employment classification, credentials, or coverage ratio. The public record does not reconcile those managers with the two-employee IRS count; managers could be volunteers, residents, or contractors.
  • The Probation agreement requires staff and volunteer background checks, appropriate training and experience, and production of employee/credential lists on request. It does not publish a minimum staffing ratio.
  • DHCS explains that sober or transitional homes providing no alcohol or drug treatment are not licensed or certified as treatment facilities by DHCS. Stalwart’s County agreements prohibit on-site treatment. Absence of a treatment-facility license is therefore not evidence of unlawful operation.
  • County contracts incorporate CCAPP recovery-residence standards. Contractual incorporation is not proof of current accreditation. Stalwart’s website references CAARR standards, but no current certificate or directory entry was independently verified.

7. Oversight and governance

Confirmed public-record concerns

  1. County risk category. A May 2023 Behavioral Wellness Board letter states that the County’s risk assessment placed Stalwart in the high-risk category. It also says Stalwart was then the only provider able to provide local recovery-residence services and was selected under public exigency. The public letter does not reveal why the risk rating was assigned.
  2. Performance shortfalls. FY2022–23 retention and case-management results were materially below contractual goals, although successful discharge exceeded its goal.
  3. Financial resilience. The FY2025 IRS filing reports negative net assets of $5,987. This indicates limited balance-sheet resilience; it is not misconduct.
  4. Governance policies. The FY2025 filing answers “no” for written conflict-of-interest, whistleblower, and document-retention policies.
  5. Family relationship. The filing reports that Steve Goralski and Marquel Goralski are married. Four voting and four independent governing members are reported, while the displayed officer/director table contains three people; the roster should be reconciled.
  6. Related-party loan entry. Schedule L contains an $8,565 year-end balance involving Executive Director Steve Goralski and reports neither board/committee approval nor a written agreement. The public rendering does not by itself establish the direction, original purpose, terms, or legality of the loan.

Controls documented in current agreements

  • monthly invoices and supporting detail;
  • client-specific service summaries for Probation;
  • traceability from transactions to billings;
  • County inspection and audit rights;
  • non-duplicated client counts for opioid-settlement invoices;
  • monitoring, site/electronic reviews, corrective-action mechanisms, withholding, recoupment, and repayment remedies; and
  • a CPA-prepared financial audit and management letter as a condition precedent to annual Probation payment.

These provisions demonstrate oversight authority. They are not evidence that a violation, corrective action, repayment, or sanction occurred.

Adverse findings not located

The three research lanes found no responsive public documentary evidence of:

  • fraud or duplicate billing;
  • questioned costs or repayment demand;
  • County sanction or completed corrective-action plan;
  • state or federal enforcement action;
  • a Stalwart-specific lawsuit;
  • unlawful lack of a treatment-facility license; or
  • independently substantiated adverse media allegations.

“Not located” is not a clean-record certification. County complaint, monitoring, invoice, local court, code, fire, and closed regulatory records are not comprehensively indexed.

  3. Reported performance: County-reported measures with definitions and limitations.

Method and limitation

Three specialized research lanes independently examined funding/entity identity, service/utilization/outcomes, and oversight/governance. The coordinating review rechecked the core dollar, bed, performance, risk-category, and Form 990 figures against the cited documents. Public indexing is incomplete, and the research did not include nonpublic claims, payment ledgers, confidential client records, sealed cases, or records not yet produced under public-records law.

This report is an accountability research document, not a legal conclusion, audit opinion, service endorsement, or finding of wrongdoing. Corrections supported by documents should be logged and published.
